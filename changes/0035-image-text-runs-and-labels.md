# 0035: Image text targets are runs of words, bounded in count, labelled beside the text

Status: proposed
Why: Task 0038. On GG's screen on 2026-10-25, two Foot windows gave 722 detector components: the detector joins text only across its own 4 unit gap, while Foot's word gap is about 7.7 units at scale 1.25, so every word stayed its own image target. The detector's 500 element cap then dropped the lowest-confidence words, which are the ones at the bottom of the screen, and 477 targets reached the overlay. 475 of them were narrower than the 18 unit chip, so REQ-LABEL-02 centred each opaque chip on the word's top-left corner, and the chips covered 35.4 percent of the text's ink. REQ-SRC-06 does not say what an image text target is or that the source is bounded in count, and REQ-LABEL-02 has no placement that leaves small text readable. On a synthetic monospace frame at Foot's spacing (in crates/thodud/src/image.rs), joining runs of words gives one target per run, 224 targets from 1021 word detections, and labels beside the text cover 5.4 percent of the ink, none of it under an opaque label, against 33.7 percent for per-word labels.

## Modified

### REQ-SRC-06: Fill accessibility gaps from the screen image
For every visible-window collection, the system shall run the bounded image detector after primary sources, including when AT-SPI found targets, so visually apparent controls omitted from an application's accessibility tree can be added. It shall capture the focused monitor once, map a detection only when a visible window owns its click point, assign it to that window, and discard candidates substantially covered by AT-SPI targets for that window, shell targets, or the overlay's rendered label chips, focus ring and status toast. It shall convert detections to logical coordinates and clip them to that window and monitor. An image text target is a run of words on one line: the system shall join a window's text detections on one line when the gap between them is no wider than that window's word gap, measured from the frame as the most common gap between neighbouring text detections on a line and never more than one and a half median text heights, unless a box detection, an AT-SPI target, a shell target or an overlay rectangle separates them or the joined run's click point is owned by another window. A gap of two or more spaces in a monospace window stays a break. The image source shall be bounded in count as well as in time: the detector shall return every detection up to a cap high enough that a screen of text reaches joining whole, and a collection shall contribute at most 500 image targets, dropping the least confident and then the latest in reading order. Image targets shall offer click only, have no invented text, and use a visibly different label style. If capture or detection fails, or exceeds the image source's 180 ms budget, it shall contribute no image targets and name the failure or timeout in status. In an interactive hints session, primary targets shall draw before image detection completes; the session shall then update to the image targets in a new generation. While image collection is pending or the overlay has not loaded the current target generation, hint keys shall not select labels and Escape shall remain available. Each key request shall include the generation shown by the overlay. The daemon shall ignore a stale-generation key other than Escape and return the current targets and view for the ignored key.
Acceptance, added:
- scenario: a synthetic monospace frame at Foot's spacing, with 7.2 and 8.0 unit word gaps, yields one image target per run of words, not per word, every run including the bottom lines, and fewer targets than the cap.
- scenario: on a Chromium-like frame, outlined buttons a word gap apart and text items far apart keep their own targets while paragraph words join into lines.
- scenario: words a word gap apart stay apart across two adjacent boxes, a small box between them, or an accessible control between them.
- scenario: more detections than the cap give exactly the cap of targets, the least confident and latest dropped.

### REQ-LABEL-02: Labels sit on their targets
The system shall place each label inside its target's bounds when the target is at least as large as the label, and otherwise centered on the target's top-left corner, clipped to the monitor. A label on an image text target (an image target with the text or link role) shall instead use a smaller label box and sit centered on the text's line, just after the text's end, or else just before its start, where that spot lies inside the target's window and overlaps no other target or label; only when neither spot is free shall it sit over the end of the text, and the overlay shall then draw it translucent so the text under it stays visible.
Acceptance:
- property: for any target and label size, the placement follows this rule.
- property: an image text label is placed after the text, else before it, else over the text's end, always on the monitor.
- scenario: on the synthetic monospace frame the labels lie over under 8 percent of the text's ink and opaque labels hide under 0.5 percent of it, measured over every ink pixel.
- scenario: the overlay draws image text labels in the smaller font, opaque beside the text and translucent over it, and the ink under a translucent label keeps over half its contrast in the rendered frame.
